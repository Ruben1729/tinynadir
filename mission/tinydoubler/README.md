# tinydoubler

Part of [Mission](../README.md) in [TinyNadir](../../README.md).

Double-r angles-only initial orbit determination solves two geocentric radii using Newton updates of conic time-of-flight residuals. It is a distinct algorithm: line-of-sight/radius intersections establish the first two positions, coplanarity establishes the third, Gibbs supplies the middle velocity, and the two conic flight times constrain the radii.

Include `tinydoubler/tinydoubler.h`; link `tinydoubler::tinydoubler`. Supply three consecutive inertial sight-line and observer-position triples, mu [m³/s²], relative observation times `tau1 < 0 < tau3` [s], and two initial **geocentric radii** [m]. The radii are seed estimates, not distances from the observer. Output is the middle Cartesian position/velocity [m,m/s], the three observer ranges, fitted radii, flight-time residuals, and iteration count. Sight lines are normalized internally.

The supported profile is elliptic eccentricity below 0.95, nonradial motion, positive ranges using the far intersection of each ray with its radius sphere, and consecutive forward minor arcs with each flight time below half the period. It excludes hyperbolic/parabolic and multiple-revolution solutions. Both seeds must initially produce admissible geometry. Multiple orbit solutions can depend on the seeds; the library imposes no hidden altitude or orbit-selection prior. Small sight-line/plane denominators, singular Newton systems, unsuitable geometry, and failure to converge are reported explicitly. Poor geometry and noisy angles still require more observations and subsequent estimation.

The solver uses a central finite-difference Jacobian with one-sided fallback, limits the Newton step, and tries up to 12 decreasing line-search steps per iteration. Callers choose 1–64 Newton iterations and a flight-time tolerance from 1e-10 to 1 second. Time residual is a numerical stopping criterion, not a position-error guarantee. There is no heap, I/O, mutable state, or external reference-library dependency; fixed local vectors/results use bounded stack and failures preserve the caller's result.

## Verification

48 synthetic observation sets were independently generated by SciPy DOP853 two-body propagation with rotating observer stations (seed 564712). Frozen middle states come from an independent NumPy conic-time evaluator fitted with **SciPy 1.18.1 `optimize.root`**, using the existing Vallado Gibbs equivalent for velocity. All fits were checked against the generating trajectory. At a 1e-9 s flight-time tolerance, sampled C differences from the independent fits were at most **8.08e-5 m** and **5.62e-7 m/s**. Static tolerances are 1e-3 m and 1e-4 m/s.

An additional 37 cases freeze agreement with `astro.iod.angles.doubler` from [CelesTrak/fundamentals-of-astrodynamics](https://github.com/CelesTrak/fundamentals-of-astrodynamics), commit **49df0479c950917c0a2c5ac31f2db6477bba57f6**. The upstream routine returned finite states for 11 additional sampled cases that failed the independent generating-trajectory checks; those unvalidated outputs were retained in the ignored comparison artifacts and were not adopted as expected answers. The independent SciPy fits and this implementation recovered all 48 sampled trajectories. This observation concerns that pinned routine and tested settings, and does not establish a cause for the upstream failures or validate every double-r branch.

Offline tests check positive ranges, converged time residuals, reference states, invalid inputs, degenerate sight geometry, and preservation of results on failure. Raw generators, full reference outputs, rejected comparisons, and verification details remain ignored under `.validation/tinydoubler/` and `.validation/adcs-reference/`; tests require neither Python nor downloaded libraries. The implementation was independently written from the two-radius/conic equations. No Vallado, NumPy, or SciPy source is copied. See `NOTICE` and `LICENSE`.

This is an initial-estimation building block. Its output can feed a two-body propagator or a later measurement fit; it is independent of TLE/SGP4 propagation.
